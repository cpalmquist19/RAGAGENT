## Policy Statement


This Policy governs requests, eligibility, and renewal of residency licenses, as well as professional development requirements for a public school unit that employs an individual holding a residency license.

## Policy

1. **Eligibility for a Residency License**
  (a) The governing body of a public school unit or an eligible nonpublic school may request a residency license for an applicant under its employment if the applicant meets the requirements of G.S. 115C-270.20(a)(5) and has either:
    (i) Completed at least 24 hours of coursework in the content area for which the license is sought; or
    (ii) Passed the applicable licensure examination requirements for that content area.
      For purposes of this Policy, an “eligible nonpublic school” is defined in G.S. 115C-207.20(a)(5)a.2.
  (b) A person who holds or previously held a residency license shall not be eligible for a permit to teach or an emergency license in the same license area.
  (c) A person who previously held a lateral entry license issued by the Department of Public Instruction under G.S. 115C-297 (repealed by S.L. 2017-189) but did not convert that license to a continuing professional license prior to its expiration shall qualify for a residency license in the same license area if the person:
    (i) Meets the requirements of G.S. 115C-270.20(a)(5); and
    (ii) Has passed the applicable licensure examination requirements for that content area.
2. **Renewal of Residency Licenses**
  (a) To renew a residency license, the PSU governing body or eligible nonpublic school employing the applicant shall provide documentation to DPI that the applicant:
    (i) Is enrolled in a recognized educator preparation program;
    (ii) Is employed as a teacher in the public school unit or eligible nonpublic school;
    (iii) Acquired at least six months of teaching experience during the preceding school year; and
    (iv) Completed 10 days of professional development, as described in Section 5 of this Policy, in the first year of the residency license.
  (b) If an applicant fails to renew a residency license for the second or third year, the residency license shall expire. A PSU governing body or eligible nonpublic school that hires the applicant within three years of the effective date of the initial residency license may request that DPI reinstate the license, provided that the license has not already been renewed twice and the PSU or eligible nonpublic school employing the applicant provides documentation that the applicant is employed by the PSU governing body or eligible nonpublic school and enrolled in a recognized educator preparation program.
3. **Conversion to an Initial or Continuing Professional License**
  (a) To convert a residency license to an IPL or a CPL, the applicant shall:
    (i) Meet the requirements for an IPL in LICN-020 or a CPL in LICN-024; and
    (ii) Be recommended by the EPP that prepared the licensee.
  (b) If the applicant satisfies the above requirements by June 30 of the third year of the residency license, the IPL or CPL shall be effective July 1 of the next fiscal year. If the applicant fails to satisfy the requirements by this date, the residency license shall expire, and the IPL or CPL shall be effective on the date the applicant satisfies the requirements.
4. **Additional License Areas** The governing body of a public school unit or eligible nonpublic school employing the applicant may request a residency license in an additional license area for an applicant under its employment if the applicant holds a clear license in another area, provided that the applicant meets the requirements of G.S. 115C-270.20(a)(5) for each of the requested areas.
5. **Professional Development Requirements**
  (a) A public school unit that employs a teacher holding a residency license shall, within the teacher’s first year on the license, provide the teacher with at least three hours of field experience before the teacher begins teaching in the classroom and at least 10 hours of field experience over the course of the year. The field experience requirement can be fulfilled while the teacher holds a permit to teach or an emergency license in advance of the first year of the residency license.
  (b) For purposes of this Policy, “field experience” means opportunities to observe, practice, and demonstrate knowledge and skills in a classroom setting in the presence of students. Field experience may include preclinical classroom experiences.
  (c) The PSU shall provide the teacher with 10 days of professional development that includes the following:
    (i) An overview of the goals, policies, and procedures of the PSU.
    (ii) An overview of the mission and goals of the State Board of Education.
    (iii) A description of services and professional development opportunities available to the teacher.
    (iv) Information on the process for obtaining a CPL.
    (v) The guidelines for optimal working conditions for all beginning teachers.
    (vi) Training on EVAAS.
    (vii) A review of the North Carolina Standard Course of Study, including end-of-grade and end-of-course testing requirements.
    (viii) A review of the PSU curriculum.
    (ix) Training on lesson planning.
    (x) Training on classroom organization.
    (xi) Training on classroom management, including:
      (A) Positive management of student behavior;
      (B) Effective communication for defusing and de-escalating disruptive or dangerous behavior; and
      (C) Safe, appropriate, and legal use of seclusion and restraint.
    (xii) An overview of the identification and education of children with disabilities.
  (d) The PSU may, in the alternative, satisfy the professional development requirements of Section a by requiring the teacher to complete the following:
    (i) The North Carolina Effective Teacher Training;
    (ii) Training on evidence-based practices as defined in 20 U.S.C. 7801(21)(A);
    (iii) Training on the Whole School, Whole Community, Whole Child Framework promulgated by the United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention;
    (iv) Training on positive management of student behavior and effective communication techniques for defusing and de-escalating disruptive and dangerous behavior; and
    (v) State required training, such as the Science of Reading.
    For teachers holding a residency license, emergency license, or permit to teach, the PSU shall provide at least eight hours of training in Subsections (2)(d)(ii) through (2)(d)(v) before the teacher begins teaching in the classroom.
  (e) The PSU may provide all 10 days of professional development pursuant to Subsection (2)(a) or (2)(b) during the preservice period before the teacher begins teaching in the classroom or may provide five days of professional development during the preservice period with the remaining five days taking place throughout the teacher’s first year on the residency license.
